# 50 news bulletins from *Nineteen Eighty-Four*

These short, randomized bulletin ideas summarize events that occur in George Orwell’s novel. They are paraphrases, not quotations. The website selects them randomly and saves each delivered bulletin in the Inbox.

1. **A city under Big Brother’s gaze** — Winston returns to Victory Mansions beneath posters of Big Brother and a telescreen that can watch and listen. _(Part One, Chapter 1)_
2. **Winston starts a private diary** — Winston writes in a blank old book from a hidden corner outside the telescreen’s view. _(Part One, Chapter 1)_
3. **The Two Minutes Hate begins** — At the Ministry, workers gather before a screen and direct their anger at Goldstein and the enemy. _(Part One, Chapter 1)_
4. **An exchange of glances** — During the Hate, Winston briefly meets O’Brien’s eye and believes they share a private understanding. _(Part One, Chapter 1)_
5. **Winston writes against Big Brother** — Winston’s diary fills with forbidden thoughts, and he realizes that keeping it is an act of rebellion. _(Part One, Chapter 1)_
6. **The Parsons children play Thought Police** — The Parsons children chase Winston with toy weapons and accuse him of being a thought-criminal. _(Part One, Chapter 2)_
7. **A dream of the Golden Country** — Winston dreams of a green landscape and a dark-haired girl, images that return to him later. _(Part One, Chapter 3)_
8. **Records are rewritten overnight** — At the Ministry of Truth, Winston receives correction orders that make old reports match the Party’s current claims. _(Part One, Chapter 4)_
9. **A ration promise is revised** — Winston alters a past announcement so the Party appears to have predicted a reduction in the chocolate ration. _(Part One, Chapter 4)_
10. **A loyal hero is invented** — Winston creates Comrade Ogilvy, a fictional model Party member, to replace a person erased from the record. _(Part One, Chapter 4)_
11. **Syme works on Newspeak** — Syme explains that the new dictionary is designed to shrink the language and make unorthodox thought harder to express. _(Part One, Chapter 5)_
12. **Lunchroom talk turns to rations** — Winston eats in the Ministry canteen as colleagues discuss shortages, production figures, and Party slogans. _(Part One, Chapter 5)_
13. **A memory of the past resurfaces** — Winston recalls the names Jones, Aaronson, and Rutherford and the evidence that once contradicted their forced confessions. _(Part One, Chapter 7)_
14. **Winston finds proof, then destroys it** — A photograph in an old newspaper briefly confirms Winston’s memory before he drops it into the memory hole. _(Part One, Chapter 7)_
15. **The proles sing in the street** — Winston watches a working-class woman singing while she hangs laundry and wonders whether ordinary people may preserve the future. _(Part Two, Chapter 4)_
16. **Winston returns to the antique shop** — Winston visits Mr Charrington’s shop and studies an old room above it, drawn to its objects from the past. _(Part One, Chapter 8)_
17. **Julia slips Winston a note** — After a fall in the Ministry corridor, Julia secretly passes Winston a message saying she loves him. _(Part Two, Chapter 1)_
18. **A meeting is arranged in the crowd** — Julia and Winston exchange directions in public and plan to meet away from the Party’s immediate notice. _(Part Two, Chapter 1)_
19. **A country rendezvous** — Julia leads Winston to a secluded countryside clearing where they can speak and be together. _(Part Two, Chapter 2)_
20. **Julia describes her private resistance** — Julia tells Winston that she has repeatedly broken Party rules while keeping an outwardly loyal appearance. _(Part Two, Chapter 3)_
21. **Winston and Julia rent the upstairs room** — The pair begin meeting in the room above Mr Charrington’s shop, believing it offers privacy from the telescreens. _(Part Two, Chapter 4)_
22. **A paperweight becomes a keepsake** — Winston buys a glass paperweight containing a piece of coral and imagines the room as a small world sealed away from the Party. _(Part Two, Chapter 4)_
23. **The old rhyme is completed** — Mr Charrington remembers more lines of a nursery rhyme Winston had heard earlier. _(Part Two, Chapter 4)_
24. **A rat frightens Winston** — A rat appears in the rented room, revealing one of Winston’s deepest fears to Julia. _(Part Two, Chapter 4)_
25. **Winston and Julia visit O’Brien** — The couple go to O’Brien’s flat after he suggests he can provide a copy of the Newspeak dictionary. _(Part Two, Chapter 8)_
26. **O’Brien speaks of the Brotherhood** — Winston and Julia tell O’Brien they oppose the Party, and he claims that a resistance organization exists. _(Part Two, Chapter 8)_
27. **A forbidden book is promised** — O’Brien arranges for Winston to receive the book attributed to the Party’s enemy, Emmanuel Goldstein. _(Part Two, Chapter 8)_
28. **The book describes the power structure** — Winston reads an account of the three superstates, their changing alliances, and the Party’s system of control. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
29. **Winston and Julia read together** — In the rented room, Winston reads Goldstein’s book aloud while Julia listens and rests nearby. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
30. **Hate Week preparations fill the city** — The Party organizes banners, songs, processions, and decorations as London prepares for Hate Week. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
31. **The hidden telescreen is revealed** — A voice from behind the picture orders Winston and Julia to stand still; the room’s apparent refuge was being watched. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
32. **The paperweight is shattered** — Thought Police officers enter the room, arrest the couple, and break the glass paperweight. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
33. **Mr Charrington’s disguise falls away** — The shopkeeper appears in a different role, revealing himself as an agent of the Thought Police. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
34. **Winston wakes in a Ministry cell** — Winston is imprisoned in the Ministry of Love, where other detainees wait under constant surveillance. _(Part Three, Chapter 1)_
35. **Parsons is arrested** — Parsons arrives in the cell after his own child reports him for speaking against Big Brother in his sleep. _(Part Three, Chapter 1)_
36. **Ampleforth is detained over a poem** — The poet Ampleforth tells Winston he was arrested after leaving a forbidden word in a verse of Kipling. _(Part Three, Chapter 1)_
37. **O’Brien appears in the prison** — Winston discovers that O’Brien is present in the Ministry of Love, but as an interrogator rather than an ally. _(Part Three, Chapter 2)_
38. **O’Brien questions Winston’s reality** — During interrogation, O’Brien insists that the Party can define reality and forces Winston to confront the number of fingers shown. _(Part Three, Chapter 2)_
39. **Winston is told the photograph was real** — O’Brien briefly shows Winston the photograph of Jones, Aaronson, and Rutherford, then removes it and denies its importance. _(Part Three, Chapter 2)_
40. **Winston is forced to face Room 101** — In Room 101, Winston panics at the prospect of rats and begs for the punishment to be transferred to Julia. _(Part Three, Chapter 5)_
41. **Winston is released** — After months of treatment, Winston is back outside the Ministry of Love and spends his days in the Chestnut Tree Café. _(Part Three, Chapter 6)_
42. **Winston and Julia meet again** — Winston encounters Julia in a park; both acknowledge that their time in custody changed their feelings for each other. _(Part Three, Chapter 6)_
43. **The war announcement changes** — During a Hate Week rally, the announced enemy switches from Eurasia to Eastasia, and the crowd immediately redirects its anger. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
44. **The Ministry corrects the enemy’s name** — Workers urgently revise posters and speeches after the Party announces the new enemy, as though the former alliance had never existed. _(Part Two, Chapter 9)_
45. **Winston studies the war bulletin** — At the café, Winston follows the telescreen’s war reports and celebrates a claimed victory in Africa. _(Part Three, Chapter 6)_
46. **Winston hears the chestnut-tree song** — A song from the telescreen recalls the betrayals of the imprisoned Party leaders and accompanies Winston’s isolation. _(Part Three, Chapter 6)_
47. **Winston works on the Newspeak dictionary** — Winston is assigned to a minor committee discussing the Eleventh Edition of the Newspeak Dictionary. _(Part Three, Chapter 6)_
48. **A final bulletin reports victory** — The telescreen announces a military success, and Winston feels renewed loyalty to Big Brother. _(Part Three, Chapter 6)_
49. **Julia brings forbidden luxuries** — Julia brings coffee, sugar, and other scarce items to the rented room, supplies unavailable through ordinary Party channels. _(Part Two, Chapter 4)_
50. **Winston’s last resistance collapses** — After his final confrontation with O’Brien, Winston no longer maintains his hatred of Big Brother. _(Part Three, Chapter 6)_

Source: [George Orwell, *Nineteen Eighty-Four* (Project Gutenberg Australia)](https://gutenberg.net.au/ebooks01/0100021h.html).
